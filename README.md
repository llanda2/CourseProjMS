# Gun Violence and Legislation Dashboard

## Overview

This dashboard explores the relationship between mass shootings in the United States, public opinion on gun control, and state-level gun legislation. It also incorporates major Supreme Court rulings related to the Second Amendment to provide broader context. The goal is to offer a clear, interactive tool for examining the frequency and distribution of mass shootings, how public sentiment changes over time, and how state laws may correlate with gun violence.

## Datasets Used

### 1. Gun Violence Archive
- **Source**: [Gun Violence Archive – Mass Shootings](https://www.gunviolencearchive.org/mass-shooting?sort=asc&order=Incident%20Date)
- **Focus**: Mass shootings, defined as incidents where four or more individuals are injured or killed.
- **Content**: Includes incident date, location, number of victims, and other incident-level information.

### 2. Gallup Opinion Polls
- **Source**: [Gallup – Guns](https://news.gallup.com/poll/1645/guns.aspx)
- **Focus**: Public opinion on gun laws, satisfaction with legislation, and personal firearm ownership.
- **Content**: Time-series data on American attitudes toward gun control.

### 3. Everytown Research Rankings
- **Source**: [Everytown – Methodology](https://everytownresearch.org/rankings/methodology/)
- **Focus**: State-by-state ranking of gun law strength and corresponding gun death rates.
- **Content**: Quantitative scores for law strength and gun mortality, with supporting documentation.

## Data Use and Licensing

- **Gallup**: Data is publicly accessible for non-commercial use with appropriate citation and annotation.
- **Gun Violence Archive**: Data is a public resource provided to inform the public and support research.
- **Everytown**: Research is freely available and intended to support evidence-based policy and advocacy.

## Project Context

### Motivation

This project is inspired by the dissertation work of Laura Muna-Landa, who is researching the Second Amendment and Supreme Court jurisprudence. As mass shootings are frequently central to Second Amendment debates, this project seeks to clarify how often these incidents occur, how they impact public opinion, and how they intersect with legal and policy changes.

### Audience

The dashboard is designed for:
- Policymakers interested in legislation and outcomes
- Academic researchers in public health, law, and political science
- Gun safety advocates and non-profit organizations
- Members of the general public interested in understanding trends in gun violence

## Key Features

- Interactive map of mass shooting incidents
- Public opinion visualizations showing trends over time
- Comparative view of gun law strength and gun death rates by state
- Timeline overlay of major Supreme Court Second Amendment cases
- Exploration of possible correlations between events and changes in sentiment or legislation

## Future Enhancements

- Add detailed views of individual incidents
- Include news or media annotations for key events
- Provide filtering by demographic or geographic criteria
- Integrate trendlines or projections for policy evaluation
